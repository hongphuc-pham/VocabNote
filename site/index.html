Schwa Notes is a small notebook for English pronunciation. Save a word with its IPA, colour the exact symbol that trips you up, leave yourself a note, and practise a few cards a day. Everything stays on your phone.

How it works

Six small things. The app's own How to use guide shows each one, with a button that takes you straight there.

Add a word

Tap + on My words and type the word you're learning. Saving takes two taps.

Fill it from the dictionary

Tap Look up to see how it sounds, what it means and an example. Nothing is filled in until you tap the part you want. Look-up is the only thing that uses the internet, and it sends only that word.

Write the IPA yourself

The row above the keyboard has every sound English needs, ə ɪ ʊ æ ɒ ʌ θ ð ʃ ʒ ŋ and the rest. Typing the IPA yourself is one of the best ways to remember it.

Highlight the sound you struggle with

Open a word's IPA and mark the sounds you keep getting wrong, in a colour and with a note to yourself.

Leave a note for yourself

Add as many notes to a word as you like: where you heard it, what your mouth should do, what to listen for.

Practise a little every day

A few flashcards a day beats a long session once a week. The words you find hard come back sooner.

Tap play to hear a word in UK or US English. The voice is your phone's own text-to-speech: a good guide to stress and vowels, but not a native speaker.

Questions

Why "schwa"?

ə, the schwa, is the most common vowel sound in English, and it is almost never spelled the way it sounds: the a in about, the er in teacher, the o in lemon. It's the classic thing a learner hears wrong, which is what this app is for.

Where are my words kept?

Only on your phone, in the app's own private storage. There is no account and no server. To keep a copy anywhere else, make a backup.

How do I move my words to a new phone?

Go to Settings → Backup & restore → Export backup, and save the file somewhere your new phone can reach. Then install Schwa Notes there and choose Import backup.

Does it need the internet?

No. Everything works offline. Only Look up uses the internet, and all it sends is the word you are looking up. You can switch look-up off in Settings.

What is IPA?

The International Phonetic Alphabet: one symbol for each sound, so a pronunciation is written down exactly, /kɒf/ rather than "coff".

How does practice decide what comes back?

Each word sits in a box. Know it and it moves up a box and waits longer; miss it and it comes back later the same day. You can change the gaps in Settings → Practice.

My phone has no British English voice.

Add one in your phone's settings, under Text-to-speech. Until then, the app uses the closest voice it can find.

Where do the definitions come from?

From Wiktionary, through FreeDictionaryAPI.com, shared under CC BY-SA 4.0. Offline pronunciations come from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary. Settings → Data sources & licences in the app has the details.

Something isn't working. Where do I tell you?

Open an issue on GitHub. The app's Settings → Help & feedback takes you there too.
