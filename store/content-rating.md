# Content rating and target audience — draft answers

Play Console → **Policy and programs → App content**.

## Content rating (IARC questionnaire)

| Question | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Category | **Reference, News, or Educational** | A vocabulary and pronunciation notebook |
| Violence, fear, sexuality, language, controlled substances, gambling | **No** to all | None in the app. Definitions come from Wiktionary, and a dictionary can define any word the user types — but only when the user looks that word up. |
| Do users interact or exchange content with each other? | **No** | Notes stay on the phone. There is no sharing between users. |
| Does the app share the user's current location with other users? | **No** | |
| Does the app allow users to purchase digital goods? | **No** | The optional Ko-fi link is a tip on a website that unlocks nothing (Google Play Payments policy: a tip of this kind is a peer-to-peer payment). |
| Does the app contain unrestricted internet access (a browser)? | **No** | Links open in the phone's own browser; the app has no web view. |

Expected result: the lowest rating in each region (e.g. PEGI 3, Everyone).

## Target audience and content

| Question | Recommended answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Target age groups | **13–15, 16–17, 18 and over** | Built for teenage and adult English learners (IELTS, work vocabulary). |
| Could the app unintentionally appeal to children? | **No** | Plain notebook design, no characters or games aimed at children. |

**Why not include under-13s:** choosing an under-13 age group enrols the app in Google Play's
Families requirements (a Families-specific privacy review, restrictions on external links and on
the dictionary source's unfiltered content). The app would likely meet most of them — it collects
nothing — but a free dictionary look-up and outbound links to Ko-fi and GitHub are exactly what
that review scrutinises. Children *can* still install it; they are not the audience it is
designed for. Revisit if a teacher or school ever asks for it.
